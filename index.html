Numerical & conservation diagnostics

The massive-body system is conservative, so its energy and momenta should be constant: drift there is integration error. The asteroid's heliocentric orbital energy is not conserved (planetary forcing, radiation) — its change is physics, not drift. The noise floor is a crude estimate (accumulated local-error estimates plus a round-off random walk); the tolerance-refinement check below is the direct test.

Methods, approximations and scope

What this is

A numerical demonstration and a comparison between model variants. It is not a fitted orbit, not an ephemeris, and it makes no claim to reproduce observations of (1620) Geographos or any other object, to match JPL Horizons, or to estimate impact probabilities. The "Geographos-inspired" preset borrows approximate published values (a ≈ 1.2455 AU, e ≈ 0.3354, i ≈ 13.3°, P ≈ 5.223 h, elongated ~5 × 2 km shape, pole near ecliptic λ ≈ 55°, β ≈ −46°); its shape, density, thermal and optical properties, epoch and phases are assumed. Planet starting states come from approximate J2000 mean elements, so the planetary configuration is a synthetic, deterministic snapshot.

Frame and gravity

All states are integrated in one inertial frame: the Solar-System barycentre with heliocentric-ecliptic J2000 axes (top-down views look down from ecliptic north). Sun, eight planets and the Moon interact through mutual Newtonian point-mass gravity. The eight asteroid copies are test particles integrated in the same state vector, so they share exactly the same planetary trajectories and step sequence. Because the frame is barycentric, third-body accelerations enter directly; switching P off removes the Earth and Moon terms from that asteroid's equation of motion (the Sun's reflex motion remains part of the shared trajectories). Heliocentric orbital elements use r − r☉, v − v☉ and μ = G M☉.

Asteroid dynamics

r̈ = Σb −G mb (r − rb)/|r − rb|³ + (Fdirect·[S] + Fthermal·[T]) / m q̇ = ½ q ⊗ (0, ω) I ω̇ = τdirect·[S] + τthermal·[T] − ω × (I ω)

q maps body to inertial axes; ω and τ are in the body (principal-axis) frame. Centre of mass and the inertia tensor are computed from the closed convex triangular mesh with uniform density (tetrahedron decomposition), and the mesh is re-expressed in its principal frame (Ix ≤ Iy ≤ Iz); the spin axis is initially the maximum-inertia axis pointing along the chosen pole. Lever arms are facet centroids relative to the centre of mass.

Direct solar radiation (per illuminated facet, cos θ = n·s > 0)

F = (I(r) A cos θ / c) [ −(α + ρd) s − (2 ρs cos θ + ⅔ ρd) n ], α + ρd + ρs = 1, I(r) = L☉ / (4π r²), τ = Σ rc × F

Absorbed and diffusely reflected light transfer the incident momentum along −s; diffuse (Lambertian) reflection adds a ⅔ recoil along −n; specular reflection gives 2 cos θ along −n. For an ideal mirror at normal incidence this reduces to F = 2IA/c (checked in verification).

Thermal recoil — explicitly simplified, finite-heat-capacity model

CA dTf/dt = α I(r) max(0, nf·s) − ε σ Tf⁴ Fthermal = Σf −(2 / 3c) ε σ Af Tf⁴ nf, τthermal = Σ rc × Ff

Each facet is a single lumped layer with heat capacity per unit area CA; there is no subsurface conduction, no lateral conduction, no self-heating between facets and no shadowing (the shape is convex). Temperatures are state variables integrated with everything else and are initialised by the documented warm-up (see the parameter panel). Facet sums are the complete representation of Yarkovsky-type forces and YORP-type torques here: no additional empirical Yarkovsky acceleration or total YORP torque is added, and both reflected-light (S) and thermal-emission (T) torques contribute to rotational evolution. Heating remains active in every run; the T switch controls only the recoil force and torque, so runs with T on and S off are diagnostic decompositions rather than physical bodies.

Numerics

Dormand–Prince 5(4) with adaptive step, FSAL, and max-norm error control using separate absolute/relative scales for positions, velocities, quaternion components, angular velocity and temperatures; the quaternion is projected to unit norm after each accepted step and the pre-projection deviation is reported as a diagnostic. The step size is dominated by the rotation and by the terminator kink in the heating term; playback speed is reduced when the hardware cannot keep up, never the tolerance. Because all eight copies share one step sequence, truncation errors are strongly correlated between runs and their differences are more accurate than the absolute trajectories; the refinement check measures this directly.

Factorial design

Three binary factors — P Earth+Moon gravity, S direct radiation force and torque, T thermal recoil force and torque — in all 2³ = 8 combinations with identical initial position, velocity, orientation, angular velocity, shape, material properties and temperatures. Solar gravity and the other planets act identically in every run. Run 111 is the most complete model implemented and serves as the reference for differences; it is not ground truth. Effects are reported as signed differences (e.g. displacement along the reference orbit); a two-factor interaction IST = y₁₁ − y₁₀ − y₀₁ + y₀₀ is nonzero when the joint response differs from the sum of the separate responses. Results are specific to the chosen asteroid, interval, observable and assumptions and are not converted into universal importance rankings.

Omitted physics

  • Relativistic corrections (Schwarzschild, Lense–Thirring), non-spherical planetary gravity (J₂ etc.), tidal and gravity-gradient torques on the asteroid.
  • Subsurface conduction / thermal inertia, lateral conduction, surface roughness (beaming), self-heating and mutual shadowing, planetary eclipses of the asteroid.
  • Perturbations by massive asteroids, solar oblateness, solar wind and Poynting–Robertson drag, reflected and thermal radiation from planets, outgassing.
  • The asteroid's own gravity on the planets (test-particle assumption); non-principal-axis initial rotation.

Display conventions

Body markers are drawn at a minimum pixel size (the enlargement factor is printed on the main view); force arrows are autoscaled with the scale printed on the close-up; the displacement view multiplies true displacements by the labelled magnification. No physical force is ever amplified.
